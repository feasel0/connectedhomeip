## 5. Bounded-Rounding Algorithm And Ties

Existing input-range rejection and LimitRange clamping remain in place. For a nonaligned supported position, the candidate calculates the ordinary nearest-grid quotient using existing half-up rounding, caps that quotient at `floor(10000 / Resolution)`, and only then multiplies by Resolution.

For valid Resolution 1..10000 and accepted position 0..10000, the addition is at most 15000; the capped product is at most 10000. Intermediates are explicitly uint32_t. An out-of-range rounded product such as 12000 is never formed. The resulting product is necessarily aligned; no endpoint scaling exception or new Resolution admission rule is added.

For aligned LimitRange endpoints, clamping first puts the request between grid members. Nearest-grid rounding then cannot escape those endpoints; the global quotient cap cannot lower a target below the valid aligned upper endpoint. Equal bounds are also covered.

Examples: 6000/10000 ->6000; 3/10000 ->9999; 9999/10000 ->9999; 10000/10000 ->10000. Interior 5149/5150/5151 at Resolution100 gives5100/5200/5200. Exact ties retain the existing upper neighbor when both neighbors are eligible: Resolution6000/request3000 ->6000, Resolution10000/request5000 ->10000. Resolution6000/request9000 instead gives6000 because12000 is not eligible. Odd resolutions retain their existing integer-distance behavior.

## 6. Delegate-Before-Validation Analysis

The old SetTargetState combines validation and mutation. Simply moving that setter before the delegate would update TargetState even when the delegate rejects the command, violating preserved failure semantics.

The local candidate therefore extracts only the existing read-only target validation into a private const member, reuses it in the setter, and calls it before HandleSetTarget. The original validator body is byte-identical after excluding its former mutation statement. The setter retains stack-lock assertion, unchanged-value fast path and attribute update after validation. No new public signature or instance data member is added.

The order is now: construct and normalize local candidate; existing field/current-state checks; read-only final-target validation; delegate; setter/storage after delegate success. Tests observe the old target inside the delegate and unchanged target after Busy maps to FAILURE, supported position10001 produces CONSTRAINT_ERROR, null CurrentState produces INVALID_IN_STATE, and invalid supported speed produces CONSTRAINT_ERROR. Pre-delegate validation is not a claim that arbitrary application-delegate side effects can be rolled back.
