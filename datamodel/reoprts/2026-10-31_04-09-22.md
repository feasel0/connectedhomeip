OffWaitTime units are **100 ms, not one second**. Writing is allowed at any time, but the timed effect belongs to Timed On followed by Delayed Off, or Delayed Off itself; ordinary Off is not automatically Delayed Off. TC-OO-2.1 saves OnTime, writes/reads/restores it, then saves OffWaitTime, writes 30 at step 9, reads exactly 30 at step 10, and restores at step 11. The YAML matches that plan. It supplies no OnWithTimedOff/On, explicit delay, zero-latency guarantee or active-Timed-On precondition. A genuinely active Delayed Off scenario would need controlled state or an agreed plan change for an exact-value assertion; that does not legalize countdown in fresh ordinary Off or justify weakening this test. The initial OnOff read is boolean-type-only; no explicit false-value trace is invented.

Worker B compiled three byte-identical extracted production methods (`SetOffWaitTime`, `UpdateTimer`, `TimerFired`) as C++17 with `-Wall -Wextra -Werror`. Stub timer/notification dependencies make this a source-mechanism probe, not full SDK/app/macOS execution. Ordinary Off: immediate 30, scheduled 100 ms, one callback gives 29 and intentionally fails the ordinary-Off invariant. On with zero OnTime, zero and 0xFFFF controls do not decrement; immediate pre-callback read remains 30. Method and owner hashes bind the probe.
